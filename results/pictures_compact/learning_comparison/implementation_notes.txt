I. Implementation details to describe in the manuscript

1. Evaluation setup: case33bw_ds with a 66-dimensional operating-condition input and a perturbation range of [-0.5, 0.5]. All methods are evaluated on the same 50 operating conditions and directions. DDCL learns a separate polygon for each of these 50 conditions; the manuscript should distinguish this setting from DDNN/PINN prediction on unseen operating conditions.

2. DDNN: N=100, 500, 1000, and 5000 denote the number of training conditions; another 50 conditions are used for validation. Each label contains 36 AC support points. The network is 66–128–72 with ReLU activation, and the predicted points form a region through their convex hull.

3. DDNN training: Adam optimizer, learning rate 1e-3, and batch size 5. Training runs for at most 10,000 steps, validates every 50 steps, and stops after 20 consecutive validations without a significant improvement. The weights with the lowest validation MSE are retained. Consequently, training time need not increase monotonically with N.

4. Baseline setup: PINN uses pretraining followed by two-stage physics-informed training, and its physics calculations are included in training time. For each operating condition, DDCL initially uses 10,000 labeled points (half feasible and half infeasible), fits for 2,500 steps per update, and performs at most 50 update rounds.

5. Cost reporting: all values in the table are in seconds. DDNN data generation includes both training and validation labels. PINN has no separate label-generation cost. Both DDCL offline entries are zero because all computation is assigned to the online phase. The 215,258.10-second value is cumulative over 50 operating conditions; its scope should be distinguished from the time for one network-based region output.

6. Timing environment: RTX 5080 and PyTorch 2.12.0.dev20260411+cu130. PINN and DDNN times measure the first complete call to the target model in an independent process and exclude model loading; DDNN includes convex-hull construction. Each method is timed once, so repeated-timing uncertainty is not reported.

II. Caption guidance

Shared by both figures: boxes span the 25th–75th percentiles, orange lines mark medians, and whiskers extend to the most extreme observations within 1.5 interquartile ranges. Scatter points show all directional evaluations, not independent training repetitions.

feasibility_error.pdf: feasibility error is the squared Euclidean projection distance from a support point of the predicted region to the true feasible region. The vertical axis is logarithmic, and each method has 500 evaluations (50 conditions × 10 directions).

coverage_rate.pdf: coverage is the predicted-to-true radial-length ratio × 100%. Each method has 5,000 evaluations (50 conditions × 100 directions). The dashed line marks 100%; values below and above 100% indicate undercoverage and overcoverage, respectively.
